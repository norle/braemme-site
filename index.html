Across Denmark, — of buffer zone along streams flagged as cultivated in spring 2025

In Denmark, farmers must leave a 2 or 3 metre buffer zone along streams uncultivated.1 These strips were screened across all of Denmark2 in aerial photos from spring 2025.3 Every number here was inferred with machine learning, so the numbers are not guaranteed to be accurate.

      A machine learning model was used to generate the flags. Each one is only a possible case, not a confirmed one, and not every flag has been checked by a person.

      1. The 2 metre buffer zone is set by the Watercourse Act (vandløbsloven § 69). GLM 4, a condition for EU farm subsidies, widens it to 3 metres and bans tilling, fertilising, spraying and growing crops in it. Both are measured from the top of the bank. See Landbrugsstyrelsen, GLM 4 – 3-metersbræmmer langs søer og vandløb. ↩
      2. The strips screened are the official maps of where the rules apply: Landbrugsstyrelsen's 2-metersbræmme and 3-metersbræmme 2026 datasets. The top of the bank is GeoDanmark's watercourse bank line (Vandløbskant), from Datafordeleren. ↩
      3. GeoDanmark's spring orthophotos (Ortofoto forår), from Dataforsyningen. ↩
